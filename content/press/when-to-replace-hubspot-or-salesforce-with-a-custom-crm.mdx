Prisma House, a London-based studio that builds custom CRMs, business systems and websites for growing companies, today published guidance for companies deciding between an off-the-shelf CRM and a custom-built system.

According to Prisma House, three signals show that a custom CRM will pay for itself: the company's process is genuinely unusual and the software forces it into someone else's workflow; per-seat licences are growing faster than the value the tool delivers; and the team works in spreadsheets around the CRM instead of inside it.

"If a standard CRM fits your sales process, use it. We say that before we quote anything," said Gianmarco Giordaniello, Founder and CEO of Prisma House. "But when your team works around the tool instead of in it, you're paying twice: once for the licence and once for the workaround."

One of the systems Prisma House has built replaced shared mailboxes, a generic CRM and spreadsheets with one system covering a shared inbox, follow-ups, the deal pipeline, outreach campaigns and invoices. Its follow-up engine notices when a client has not replied, drafts the next message and queues it for human approval; nothing is sent on its own. The system is shown, with fictional demo data, at [prisma-house.com/work/crm](https://prisma-house.com/work/crm).

Prisma House builds custom CRMs in five steps: mapping how work flows today, building the system around that flow, integrating it with the tools the company keeps such as email, accounting and calendars, onboarding the team, and evolving the system as the business changes. Clients own the code, the data and the hosting.
